A growth chart for a paper

Pediatricians read a child's height against percentile curves for their age. A paper's citations can be read the same way — but with a twist a growth chart doesn't have: where a paper sits today is not where it will end up. That gap is the whole problem QaL is built to measure.

I · the shape of the field

A raw citation count is almost meaningless on its own.

Half the field is never cited. Here is the cohort's own distribution — the percentile bands of cumulative citations as papers age. The median line never leaves zero; the 99th percentile climbs to 122. And one real paper (blue) sits at 1,759, so far above the field it needs a log scale to share the page.

This extreme skew is exactly why an absolute count can't be compared across papers, let alone across fields. The fix is to stop reading the count and start reading the rank.

II · the growth chart

Read the percentile, and every paper fits on one chart.

Now the y-axis is a paper's standing within its field — its percentile among the same 24,791 peers. The shaded tiers are the percentile bands (like the curves on a pediatric chart). Some papers track a band; others cross them. Toggle the archetypes and watch the sleeper climb from below the median to the 99th.

Within-field percentile by age the CDC-style view · click a paper to toggle

Band-crossing is the story. A child mostly tracks their percentile; papers do not. The sleeper is uncited for two full years, then overtakes almost everyone — the phenomenon a single early cutoff (accept/reject, an early citation count) would miss entirely.

III · deciding under uncertainty

QaL forecasts the final rank — and says how sure it is.

QaL is a belief about where a paper will eventually stand, formed from the evidence so far. The green band is the 90% interval for the eventual (10-year) percentile, re-estimated at each age from how similar papers turned out. It starts wide and narrows as the paper matures — the honest picture, and the case for deciding late.

Forecast of the eventual percentile

How this was built. Every number is real. Cohort: all Management Science & Operations Research papers (OpenAlex subfield 1803) published 2011–2014 with citation histories, pooled by paper age — papers (a deterministic 25% hash sample of the ~99k full set; bands are stable at this size). Cumulative citations come from OpenAlex counts_by_year. The percentile is a full-population mid-rank within the cohort at each age. The forecast interval is the empirical 5th–50th–95th percentile of eventual standing among papers with the same observed citations at that age — the back-tested core of QaL's calibrated Layer B (the production metric adds a conformal correction for guaranteed coverage). Archetypes were selected by trajectory shape, not cherry-picked by name.
